# condKL against PSIP, SOGP, and GVS

Data seeds [0, 1, 2]. Every method is scored at k = 1,…,32. Numbers are cached in `compare.json`.

PSIP follows Uhrenholt et al. The subset ELBO is the sum in their equation (2), not a mean. Training uses their synthetic schedule: 200 pre-training steps, 600 point-process steps with four subset samples, and 200 post-training steps on one draw from the inclusion distribution. Gaussian parameters use Adam at 0.01 and inclusion logits use 0.2. The prior weight is chosen from {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1} by validation NLPD of the unconstrained MAP set; 0.05 is the value fixed in their synthetic experiment. MAP keeps exactly the points with inclusion probability at least 1/2. An empty MAP set is left empty. Every reported MAP row satisfies E[N] ≥ k/2. The budget curve ranks those probabilities and is an adaptation: points with near-zero probability were barely trained, so a large-budget gap is not evidence against the adaptive method. The point-process gradient matches a finite difference of equation (4); the normalizer log C has no variational parameters and does not change the updates.

SOGP follows Csató and Opper. Each budget is a separate online pass through the training inputs with that basis capacity. When the basis grows past the capacity, the lowest-scoring point is deleted and α, C, and Q are updated. The kernel and noise are the ones learned by the pretrained full sparse GP.

GVS orders K(Z, Z) by pivoted Cholesky. Z, m, and S are the trained condKL model, so the comparison changes only the order. Using those shared-posterior prefixes is an adaptation of greedy variance selection.

↓ lower is better. **Bold** is the best mean in that row. A negative difference favors condKL.
